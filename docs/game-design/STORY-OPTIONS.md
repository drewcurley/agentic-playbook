# Runewell — story decisions for creator review

**Status:** The creator selected continuation A (Contain the breach) and resurrection R1 (Checkpoint anchors). B, C, R2, and R3 remain unselected alternatives preserved for reference. The 2–4 hour scope is confirmed. Detailed lore and implementation choices remain open where identified below.

## Continuation A — Contain the breach (selected)

**Intent:** Finish a compact local story while preserving the larger mystery of the protagonist.

**Proposed sequence:** The crypts provide access into the keep and evidence of the last protective effort. The player retrieves the Scepter from within the keep, uses it to pass the golem and protective door, and reaches the failing boundary around the original breach. The climax is the dangerous work of restoring that boundary. The final scene returns attention to Tyrus: the immediate flow of decay slows, but the city is not magically restored and its dead do not return.

**Proposed payoff:** The player achieves a concrete goal—containing the immediate threat—while the identity of the caller and the full meaning of Void Spawn remain unresolved. Kaelin can acknowledge the achievement without suddenly explaining every mystery.

**Selected direction:** The protection is failing; the Scepter is used to restore containment; the crypts provide a route toward the keep; the player can undertake restoration. **Details still requiring approval:** The exact mechanism, door geography, limits of the Scepter's restorative use, and why the player chooses containment. A functional motive for undertaking containment must be supplied in dialogue or discovered evidence rather than assumed from amnesia.

**Encounter implication:** Keep the sewer Guardian and add one approved crypt/keep encounter. The golem can remain an intimidating recognition encounter with the Scepter, preserving its established rule. A conventional final boss is optional and would need its own narrative approval; do not make the golem attack the recognized Scepter holder for convenience.

**Tradeoff:** Strong local closure and modest lore expansion; protagonist identity remains a deliberate thread for later development. This is the recommended starting direction for a short first game, if that degree of unresolved personal mystery fits the creator's intent.

## Continuation B — Recover the missing self

**Intent:** Make the protagonist's personal mystery the central payoff.

**Proposed sequence:** The crypts contain evidence that earlier visitors carried related traces of the same presence. In the keep, the Scepter preserves an impression of the events surrounding the city's fall. Passing the golem grants access to that evidence. At the deeper threshold, the protagonist discovers that part of their own identity originates from beyond the seal. The final decision is whether to answer the call and reunite with that origin or reject it and remain an independent person.

**Proposed payoff:** A direct answer to “not all of you has been here before,” and a personal choice with two short ending variations in one final space.

**New lore requiring approval:** A divided/fragmentary identity, its relationship to earlier visitors, the Scepter's memory-bearing capability, and the nature of the origin. It must not automatically be the forgotten god, evil, or the source of every voice. Whether either choice repairs the city's condition needs a separate decision.

**Encounter implication:** A final confrontation can defend the threshold or test access to the memory; its actor and purpose remain to be approved. The Scepter still passes the golem. Do not convert Kaelin into a deceiver merely to manufacture a twist.

**Tradeoff:** Strong personal resolution, but the largest commitment to the protagonist's hidden identity. Highest risk of contradicting a story the creator already has in mind.

## Continuation C — Release the bound voices

**Intent:** Center the ending on the human cost of the kingdom's attempted salvation.

**Proposed sequence:** The crypts reveal that the protective effort still depends on trapped remnants of people. In the keep, the Scepter grants passage past the golem to the protective door. Evidence there connects some of the pit voices to those remnants. The player can preserve the protection temporarily or release the bound people while undertaking a dangerous physical closure of the breach.

**Proposed payoff:** The “Join us” sequence becomes a meaningful clue. The ending contrasts continued containment at personal cost with an attempted release and closure. The prison helper can remain separate and unexplained.

**New lore requiring approval:** The identity of some pit voices, continued binding of people, how that binding relates to the prophets' voluntary sacrifice, and a feasible way to close the breach without them. The alternative closure must be established before the final decision; no unexplained last-minute power.

**Encounter implication:** Shared final arena and assets can support both choices. The challenge can involve reaching and operating a closure mechanism under pressure; the exact threat needs approval. Neither route inherently requires killing the golem.

**Tradeoff:** Strong emotional and thematic ending, but adds moral and supernatural rules not present in the uploads. Two branches require more writing and state testing. Release is not automatically labeled the morally correct option.

## Resurrection R1 — Checkpoint anchors (selected)

**Proposed explanation:** Prepared anchors can restore the protagonist's bodily form while an intact connection persists. Only designated sites work. This property is associated with the protagonist's unusual nature; its origin can remain unknown.

**Gameplay fit:** Conventional checkpoint return, stable world progression, no global rewind. Supplies and ordinary encounter resets can remain abstractions rather than literal resurrection of every enemy.

**Selected direction:** Prepared anchors restore this protagonist after death. **Details still requiring approval:** How their connection is established, their origin and presentation, and whether Kaelin understands them. Using her blue rune stone as the binding tool would be an additional approved power, not an existing property.

**Cost:** New checkpoint props and a short explanation. Strongest fit for a compact game without committing to time travel. Recommended mechanical fit, independently of continuation choice.

## Resurrection R2 — Local temporal recall

**Proposed explanation:** The protagonist can return to a previously fixed moment after death. This is an effect associated with time distortion in the valley, without confirming the forgotten god's identity or involvement.

**Gameplay fit:** Death causes a local rewind. Resource, enemy, key, dialogue, and door persistence must follow explicitly chosen rules; selective persistence needs an explanation the player can understand.

**Approval needed:** Time distortion is real; who remembers; which people/events rewind; why approved progression survives. The god's time domain alone does not establish any of these powers.

**Cost:** Most demanding consistency rules. Avoid unless time and memory are intended to be central to the story.

## Resurrection R3 — Borrowed bodies

**Proposed explanation:** The protagonist is a persistent consciousness capable of inhabiting another prepared vessel after a body's destruction. Checkpoints house a limited visual representation of this process; repeated retries do not require an actual finite life counter.

**Gameplay fit:** Return in a new vessel with retained progression. Ordinary enemy resets remain a separate abstraction. Cosmetics and carried unique items need explicit continuity rules.

**Approval needed:** This defines a major aspect of Void Spawn identity, establishes vessels and transfer, and determines what the player considers “self.” Do not infer it from “not all of you.”

**Cost:** Strong thematic identity, but substantially constrains future character revelations. More narrative and visual work than R1.
